The serval, a medium-sized African wild cat, has unusually large ears relative to its head, which help it detect prey moving in tall grass.
Big cats such as lions and tigers generally have round pupils, while many smaller cats, including the domestic cat, have vertical slit pupils, a difference researchers have linked to hunting style and body size.
Unlike domestic cats, lions and tigers cannot purr continuously, due to differences in the structure of a bone near the voice box called the hyoid.
Big cats such as lions, tigers, leopards, and jaguars can roar, a trait tied to a flexible hyoid structure not found in most smaller cats, including the domestic cat.
The clouded leopard has proportionally the longest canine teeth relative to skull size of any living cat species.
Jaguars have one of the strongest bite forces relative to body size among all cats, strong enough to pierce turtle shells and crocodilian skulls.
Wild cat species are found on every continent except Australia and Antarctica within their native ranges, though domestic cats have since been introduced to both.
The fishing cat, found in parts of South and Southeast Asia, has partially webbed paws and readily hunts in and around water.
Most wild cat species, like most domestic cats, are primarily solitary and territorial outside of mating and raising young.
Leopards are known for their strength and often drag prey heavier than themselves up into trees to keep it away from scavengers.
The margay, a small wild cat native to Central and South America, can rotate its ankles nearly 180 degrees, allowing it to climb down trees headfirst.
Most cats, including the domestic cat, cannot easily climb down a tree headfirst because their claws curve the wrong way for that motion, and typically back down instead.
Cats and other Felidae species share a common evolutionary lineage stretching back tens of millions of years, with the family diversifying significantly over that time.
Many wild cat species are threatened by habitat loss, and several, including the tiger and snow leopard, are classified as endangered or vulnerable.
Cats, both wild and domestic, are generally obligate carnivores, distinguishing the family from omnivorous relatives among other carnivoran groups like bears.
A group of cats is called a clowder, while a group of kittens is sometimes called a kindle or litter.
An unspayed adult female cat is called a queen, and an unneutered adult male is called a tom.
A young cat is called a kitten, typically until around one year of age, when it's generally considered an adult.
Cats are one of the most popular pets in the world, with pet cat populations numbering in the hundreds of millions globally.
In many countries, including the United States, there are more pet cats than pet dogs, partly because cat-owning households often keep more than one cat.
Cats can jump remarkably high relative to their size, often clearing heights several times their own body length in a single leap.
A cat's average top running speed is around 30 miles per hour over short distances, though this varies by individual fitness and breed.
Feral and community cat populations are often managed through trap-neuter-return, or TNR, programs, which aim to humanely control population growth.
Cats' impact on wildlife, particularly birds and small mammals, is a significant conservation concern in many regions, especially where cats have been introduced to island ecosystems.
The word cat traces back through Old English and Latin, with related forms appearing in many other European languages, suggesting a very old and widely shared root word.
Cats can produce a wide variety of distinct vocal sounds beyond simple meows and purrs, including chirps, trills, growls, hisses, and yowls.
Hissing is a defensive sound thought to have evolved partly to mimic the warning hiss of a snake and startle a potential threat.
Cats' body temperature and metabolism make them more sensitive to certain medications and toxins than dogs of a similar size.
Some cats show a strong attraction to catnip, a plant containing a compound called nepetalactone that can trigger playful or relaxed behavior, though not all cats are affected, due to a genetic sensitivity.
Cats that don't respond to catnip may instead respond to other plants with a similar effect, such as silver vine or valerian root.
Sensitivity to catnip is inherited, and kittens younger than about three to six months typically don't react to it at all.
Cats can rotate their ears independently of each other, letting them track two different sound sources at once.
Cats can hear a wider range of frequencies than humans, including some ultrasonic sounds used by rodents, which helps with hunting.
A cat's sense of smell relies on roughly 200 million scent receptors, compared with only about 5 to 6 million in the human nose.
Despite having more scent receptors than most dogs, cats generally have fewer than scent-specialist dog breeds like bloodhounds, which can have up to 300 million.
Cats rely heavily on scent, not just sight, to recognize familiar people, animals, and places.
Multi-cat households often need separate feeding areas and litter boxes, since cats can become stressed by having to share limited resources.
Veterinarians generally recommend one litter box per cat in a household, plus one extra, to reduce territorial stress.
A cat's grooming routine can take up several hours of its day, split across multiple shorter sessions rather than one long one.
Some cats show a strong dislike of water, though this isn't universal; breeds like the Turkish Van, and individual cats of any breed, can enjoy water play.
Cats don't always land on their feet; very short falls don't give a cat enough time to complete its righting reflex, which is part of why short falls can sometimes be more dangerous than longer ones.
Cats can suffer heatstroke, particularly flat-faced breeds, and need access to shade, water, and cool areas in hot weather.
Selective breeding for extreme traits, such as very flat faces or very short legs, has raised welfare concerns among veterinarians and animal welfare organizations.
Cat allergies in humans are usually triggered by a protein called Fel d 1, found in a cat's saliva, skin, and sebaceous glands, rather than by the fur itself.
Because Fel d 1 spreads through grooming, even hairless cat breeds like the Sphynx can trigger allergic reactions in sensitive people.
Some studies have linked cat ownership to a lower long-term risk of certain cardiovascular problems, though researchers note this association doesn't prove that owning a cat directly causes better heart health.
Regularly bathing a cat or using specialized wipes can help reduce, but not fully eliminate, the amount of allergen a cat sheds into its environment.
Male cats tend to produce more Fel d 1 protein than female cats, and neutered males typically produce less than unneutered males.
Toxoplasma gondii, a parasite sometimes carried by cats, is one reason pregnant women are commonly advised to avoid cleaning litter boxes, since infection can pose risks to a developing fetus.
Most human toxoplasmosis infections actually come from undercooked meat or contaminated soil rather than direct contact with cats.
Cat scratch disease, caused by a bacterium called Bartonella henselae, can be transmitted through a scratch or bite, most often from a kitten.
Washing a scratch or bite promptly and keeping cats' claws trimmed can help lower the risk of cat scratch disease.
Regular flea prevention for cats also helps reduce the risk of cat scratch disease, since fleas play a role in spreading the bacteria between cats.
Newborn kittens are entirely dependent on their mother for warmth, feeding, and stimulation to use the bathroom during their first few weeks of life.
Kittens typically begin walking, in an unsteady way, at around two to three weeks old.
A kitten's baby teeth usually start coming in around two to four weeks of age and are fully replaced by permanent teeth by about six months old.
Kittens begin the weaning process, transitioning from milk to solid food, at around four weeks old, though full weaning often isn't complete until around eight weeks.
Play behavior in kittens ramps up significantly between roughly seven and fourteen weeks of age, a period important for developing coordination and social skills.
The period between about two and seven weeks of age is considered a critical socialization window, during which kittens exposed to gentle human handling tend to grow into friendlier adult cats.
Kittens are usually not fully independent from their mother and littermates until at least eight to twelve weeks of age.
Many veterinarians and breeders recommend keeping kittens with their mother and littermates until at least twelve weeks old to support healthy behavioral development.
A kitten's adult coat color and pattern isn't always obvious at birth, since coloring can continue to develop and change slightly over the first several months.
Kittens reach roughly adult size, though not necessarily full physical maturity, by around one year of age for most breeds.
Larger breeds, such as the Maine Coon, can continue growing and filling out until they are three to five years old.
Domestic cats retain strong natural hunting instincts even when they're well-fed pets, which is why many cats still stalk toys, insects, or moving objects.
Outdoor and feral cats are highly effective predators, and studies in several countries have found that free-roaming cats kill large numbers of birds and small mammals each year.
Cats often play with captured prey before killing it, a behavior thought to reduce the risk of injury from a still-struggling animal by tiring it out first.
A cat's hunting technique typically relies on stealth and a short, explosive burst of speed rather than a long chase, unlike some pack-hunting predators.
Cats use their whiskers, night vision, and acute hearing together to detect and track prey, especially in low light.
Bell collars are sometimes used by owners specifically to help reduce a cat's hunting success rate on local wildlife.
A cat's hunting instinct is largely separate from hunger; even a well-fed cat will often still stalk, chase, and play with small moving objects.
Outdoor cats can dramatically expand their hunting range at night, when their superior low-light vision gives them an advantage over many prey species.
Cats are ambush predators by nature, generally preferring to stalk and pounce rather than run down prey over long distances.
Cats generally have better low-light vision than dogs, needing less ambient light to see effectively at night.
Dogs generally have a stronger overall sense of smell in terms of raw receptor count, but researchers have found cats may be better at distinguishing between very similar scents.
Unlike most dog breeds, cats weren't selectively bred over thousands of years for specific working roles, part of why domestic cat breeds show less physical variation than dog breeds.
Cats' body size varies far less between breeds than dogs' does; the largest and smallest cat breeds differ by a much smaller margin than the largest and smallest dog breeds.
Cats and dogs both experience REM sleep and are believed to dream, based on brain activity patterns observed in sleep studies.
Unlike dogs, cats were never bred into dramatically different working roles such as herding, guarding, or retrieving, since their usefulness to humans centered mainly on pest control.
Cats typically reach behavioral maturity faster than most dog breeds, often settling into adult temperament patterns within their first one to two years.
Chocolate and cinnamon coat colors, seen in breeds like the Havana Brown, are recessive genetic variations of black rather than separate pigments.
A cat's coat pattern and personality aren't scientifically linked in any confirmed way, despite popular claims about certain coat colors having typical temperaments.
Point coloration, seen in breeds like the Siamese, develops more strongly on cooler body parts because the responsible enzyme works better at lower skin temperatures.
Siamese kittens are often born very pale and develop their darker point coloring gradually over the first several weeks and months of life.
Albino cats, while rare, lack pigment entirely and often have pink or very pale blue eyes due to the absence of melanin.
White fur in cats can result from several different genetic mechanisms, including a dominant white gene that is distinct from albinism.
A cat's genetic sex is determined the same way as in humans, with XX typically producing females and XY typically producing males.
Millions of cats enter animal shelters in the United States each year, and spay and neuter programs are widely promoted as a way to help reduce shelter intake over time.
Black cats are sometimes reported by shelters to have longer average stays before adoption, a pattern some shelters attribute partly to lingering superstition, though data on this varies by region and study.
Community, or barn cat, adoption programs place cats with strong hunting instincts, who may not thrive as lap cats, into working roles like rodent control on farms and in warehouses.
Microchipping is one of the most reliable ways to reunite a lost cat with its owner, since collars and tags can fall off or be removed.
Many animal welfare organizations recommend keeping cats indoors, or providing a secure outdoor enclosure, to protect both the cat and local wildlife.
Many cats show a preference for drinking from a moving water source, such as a dripping tap or a pet fountain, possibly because moving water seems fresher to their instincts than standing water.
Cats often prefer their water bowl to be placed away from their food bowl, reflecting an instinct to avoid contaminating a water source near a kill site.
Cats get a meaningful portion of their daily water intake from wet food, one reason cats fed only dry food sometimes need extra encouragement to drink enough water.
Cats can develop strong food preferences early in life, and sudden changes in diet, texture, or brand are sometimes met with resistance, a pattern researchers link to a cautious feeding instinct.
Kittens learn some food preferences partly by watching what their mother eats.
Interactive toys that mimic the unpredictable movement of prey tend to hold a cat's attention longer than toys that move in a simple, repetitive pattern.
Puzzle feeders, which require a cat to work for its food, are often recommended by veterinarians as a way to provide mental stimulation and slow down fast eaters.
Scratching posts serve multiple purposes for cats: stretching the back and shoulder muscles, shedding old claw sheaths, and marking territory.
Vertical space, like cat trees and shelves, is important to many cats because height offers both a vantage point and a sense of safety.
Antifreeze has a sweet taste appealing to many animals and is highly toxic to cats even in small amounts, making it a common cause of accidental poisoning.
Long-haired cat breeds generally require more frequent brushing than short-haired breeds to prevent painful mats from forming in their coat.
Regular nail trims can help reduce damage to furniture and lower the risk of a claw becoming painfully overgrown, especially in less active indoor cats.
Cats are obligate carnivores, requiring animal protein for essential nutrients.
Preferred meats include chicken, turkey, beef, and fish such as salmon and tuna.
Wet food is often preferred due to its strong smell, moisture content, and texture.
Wet food helps cats stay hydrated, as they naturally have a low thirst drive.
Freeze-dried meat treats and small portions of cooked meat are common favorites.
Adult cats are typically lactose intolerant and should avoid cow's milk.
Toxic foods for cats include onions, garlic, grapes, raisins, and chocolate.
Effective play mimics the predatory sequence: stare, stalk, pounce, and kill.
Toys should be moved like prey, scurrying across the floor or fluttering out of sight.
Wand toys are ideal for simulating birds or rodents while keeping hands safe.
Kicker toys allow cats to grab with front paws and kick with back legs.
Laser pointers provide rapid movement but should end on a physical toy or treat.
Short play sessions lasting 10 to 15 minutes once or twice daily match their energy bursts.
Allowing cats to successfully catch the toy builds confidence and prevents frustration.
Cats are crepuscular, meaning they are most active during dawn and dusk.
Purring occurs at frequencies between 25 and 150 Hertz, which may aid tissue and bone healing.
Cats purr for contentment, but also to soothe themselves when stressed or in pain.
Whiskers are sensitive tactile organs that measure openings and detect airflow changes.
Adult cats primarily meow to communicate with humans rather than other cats.
Rubbing their face on objects or people deposits scent marks to establish familiar territory.
